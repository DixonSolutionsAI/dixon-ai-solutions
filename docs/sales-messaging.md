# InstantReply sales messaging and evaluation guide

## Positioning

**InstantReply is a draft-first support AI concept designed to help agents prepare context-aware customer replies while keeping people responsible for review and the final response.**

Lead with the operating model, not an unsupported “best in the world” claim. The defensible distinction is the focus on human-reviewed reply drafting rather than implying universal autonomous resolution. Verify actual product behavior before presenting any capability as available today.

## Message pillars

1. **Agent assistance:** A useful starting draft for repetitive support conversations.
2. **Human accountability:** Agents review, edit, escalate, and own the final answer.
3. **Evidence before scale:** Validate quality, time saved, and workflow fit with representative conversations and a baseline.
4. **Specificity over broad promises:** Confirm channels, integrations, security, pricing, and implementation details for each buyer.

## Discovery questions

- Which request types consume the most agent time?
- What is your current first-response time and how do you measure it?
- Which conversations should always require a human decision?
- What systems contain the relevant ticket and customer context?
- Which channels and support platforms must be supported on day one?
- What are your security, retention, access-control, and compliance requirements?
- How do you currently measure reply quality, policy adherence, and customer satisfaction?
- What would a successful pilot need to prove, and who will approve it?

## Objection handling

**“Will this replace our agents?”**  
“Our proposed workflow is draft-first: agents review and own what goes to customers. Let’s identify repetitive cases where assistance could help, and keep human judgment for exceptions. We should not promise staffing or cost savings before measuring a pilot.”

**“How do we know it won’t make things up?”**  
“No generative system should be assumed error-free. We should test it against your real ticket examples, score factual and policy errors, and define when agents must edit or escalate.”

**“Does it integrate with our help desk?”**  
“We need to confirm your exact platform, connector availability, required permissions, and implementation effort. We won’t claim an integration until that is verified.”

**“Is our customer data safe, and is it compliant?”**  
“We’ll provide the current security and data-handling documentation for your review. We won’t claim a certification or compliance status unless it has been independently verified and is applicable to your use case.”

**“What ROI should we expect?”**  
“We shouldn’t invent a savings or resolution-rate figure. Let’s baseline handling time, response time, edit rate, and quality for a defined ticket set, then compare pilot results.”

**“Why not use our existing platform’s AI?”**  
“That may be the right choice, especially if you need deep native automation. We should compare both options on your use cases, setup effort, review controls, quality, integration coverage, and total cost.”

## Suggested pilot measures

Track a pre-agreed, representative ticket sample and compare against a baseline:

- Time to produce a usable first draft
- Agent edit and rejection rates
- Factual and policy error rates from human review
- Escalation appropriateness
- Agent satisfaction and customer outcomes where measurable
- Setup effort, usage costs, and operational overhead

Do not present pilot results as general guarantees. State the sample, period, and measurement method.

## Claims to verify before using publicly

Do not make “best,” “most accurate,” “fastest,” guaranteed ROI, autonomous-resolution, supported-channel, named integration, security, compliance, or customer-result claims unless the capability and evidence are confirmed. The current repository is a marketing site, not proof of a functioning support-agent product or integrations.
